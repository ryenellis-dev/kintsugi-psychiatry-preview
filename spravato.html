What is Spravato?

The facts, in plain language

Spravato (esketamine) is a prescription nasal spray for depression. It comes from ketamine, and works differently from pills like SSRIs.

Clinical review: please have one of your prescribers read this whole page and correct anything before launch.

FDA approvedin 2019 for adults with treatment-resistant depression.
Also approvedfor adults with depression and suicidal thoughts or actions, together with an antidepressant.
Alone or with a pillIt can be used with an oral antidepressant, or on its own for treatment-resistant depression.

Who it's for

Adults whose depression hasn't improved enough

"Treatment-resistant" usually means you've tried at least two antidepressants, at a good dose for long enough, without enough relief.

Spravato is not a fit if you have:

  • An aneurysm or blood-vessel disease in the brain, chest, belly, arms or legs
  • An abnormal connection between arteries and veins (AVM)
  • A past bleed in the brain
  • An allergy to esketamine, ketamine, or anything in the spray

We review your full health history at your evaluation, including blood pressure, heart health, pregnancy and substance use.

A treatment day

What a visit looks like

  1. Before
    Skip food for 2 hours and drinks for 30 minutes before your visit. This helps with nausea. Arrange a ride home.
  2. Arrive
    We check your blood pressure and how you're feeling.
  3. Your dose
    You use the nasal spray yourself, with a clinician beside you. It takes a few minutes.
  4. Rest · 2 hours
    You relax in a quiet room. We check on you and your blood pressure until you're ready to go.
  5. After
    Someone else drives you home. No driving or operating machinery until the next day, after a full night's sleep.

Safety

Side effects

Most side effects start soon after the dose and fade the same day. This is why you stay with us for 2 hours.

The most common:

  • Feeling disconnected or "floaty" (dissociation)
  • Dizziness, sleepiness, or spinning (vertigo)
  • Nausea or vomiting
  • Higher blood pressure for a short time
  • Numbness, anxiety, or feeling drunk

Important safety information

Spravato carries a boxed warning for sedation, dissociation, breathing problems, abuse and misuse, and suicidal thoughts and behaviors. Because of these risks it is only given in certified offices under the FDA's Spravato REMS program, and patients are monitored after each dose.

Tell us right away if your depression worsens or you have thoughts of suicide. If you are in danger, call or text 988 or call 911.

FAQ

Common questions

Is Spravato the same as ketamine?
No. Esketamine is a close relative of ketamine. Spravato is FDA approved for depression, comes as a nasal spray, and is only given in certified offices. Ketamine infusions for depression are a different, off-label treatment.
Can I take it at home?
No. Spravato is never sent home with patients. Every dose is taken in our office, with monitoring afterward.
How soon might I feel a difference?
Some people notice changes within the first weeks of treatment. Everyone is different, and we check in with you at each visit.
Do I need to stop my current antidepressant?
Not usually. Spravato is often used together with an oral antidepressant. Don't stop or change any medication without talking to your prescriber.
Can I drive myself?
No. You need a ride home after every visit, and you shouldn't drive until the next day after a full night's sleep.
Is it covered by insurance?
Many plans cover Spravato, usually after a prior authorization. See insurance information.
